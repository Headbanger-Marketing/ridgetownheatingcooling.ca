FAQs

HVAC Questions From Ridgetown Homeowners

The things Chatham-Kent homeowners ask us most, about propane and oil heat, heat-pump conversions, ductwork, and what it is like working with a local crew.

Can you service the propane or oil furnace on my farm near Ridgetown?

Yes. Much of rural Chatham-Kent runs on delivered fuel, and our technicians work on propane and oil furnaces every day. We coordinate with your fuel supplier when needed, so a tank that runs dry in January does not leave you cold.

How fast can you get to a no-heat emergency in Dresden or Wallaceburg?

Quickly. Because we are based on Main Street East in Ridgetown, a technician can reach Dresden, Wallaceburg, Bothwell, or Highgate without the drive time of an out-of-town chain. Emergency heating calls take priority, especially during cold snaps.

I want to switch off propane or oil. Can you install a cold-climate heat pump?

Yes. We size and install cold-climate heat pumps that carry the full heating load through a Chatham-Kent winter, handle the old propane tank and oil furnace removal, and complete the rebate paperwork for the conversion.

My house is one of the older Victorians near downtown Ridgetown. Do you clean ductwork in homes like mine?

All the time. Century homes around downtown Ridgetown have ducts that have collected decades of dust, pet hair, and construction debris. We seal and pressurize each vent so the debris goes into our vacuum, not back into your rooms.

Can you install a ductless mini-split in a farm workshop or addition?

Yes. Mini-splits are a common fit for outbuildings, workshops, and additions across rural Chatham-Kent, with no ductwork required. We size the BTUs to the space so the unit actually dehumidifies in summer and heats properly through the winter.

Will a smart thermostat actually lower my delivered-fuel bills?

If it is wired and programmed correctly, yes. Propane and oil are not cheap, and a thermostat matched to your system, with sensible schedules set, is one of the fastest ways to cut what you burn through a long Chatham-Kent winter.
